pre-specified margins (`src/config.py`). Regenerate with `SIM_RUN=SIMULATION_20260930_105744 python3 src/run_all.py`.
Outputs: `logs/20260930_105744/`, `figures/20260930_105744/`.

## Results

**Integrity.** Clean: 2,202 visits and 163 trips per replica, no duplicates or missing values, flow conservation holds in every visit, no capacity violations; 20,349 visits at capacity (0.92 %).

**Occupancy (475 strata).**
- Mean W1 against `known_data` 4.54 pax (CI 4.51–4.58) against a 2 pax tolerance; 27.4 % of strata within it by W1, 65.9 % reach BC ≥ 0.97 (64.8 % without the terminals, the pipeline convention).
- Mean signed error −3.2 pax (underestimation); SD error −0.23 pax (the spread is right on average).
- Error grows with demand and with distance from the terminals: low-demand W1 2.5 (58 % within 2 pax), high-demand 5.8 (5 %), mean ≥ 40 pax 6.2 (1 %); departure terminal 2.0, mid-route 5.4.
- Worst strata: slot 23 at toA stops 3995, 3991, 5939, 4188 and toB 1167, 2538 (simulated mean about 14 against 32 pax empirical, W1 17–19) and slot 5 at toB 2538, 1167 (W1 18).

**Travel times.** 464 strata: MAE 2.17 s (CI 2.07–2.27), mean error +0.09 s, 95 % interval coverage 94.9 %.

**Operational risk.** Occupancy exceedance is underestimated in the middle of the distribution and overestimated in the tail:

| c | empirical | C engine | sim − emp |
|---|---|---|---|
| 40 | 24.6 % | 18.1 % | −6.5 pp |
| 60 | 5.6 % | 5.2 % | −0.4 pp |
| 70 | 1.9 % | 2.6 % | +0.7 pp |
| 80 | 0.51 % | 1.00 % | +0.49 pp |

With the 0.10 rule at 60 pax the model flags 77 strata against 98 empirically (agreement 93.1 %, κ = 0.77, 27 false negatives mostly in slots 17–21, 6 false positives, top-10 overlap 8/10, Spearman 0.95). Denied boardings (no empirical reference): 17.9 visits and 177 passengers per day.

**Robustness.** The same conclusions in all 16 groups and for all metrics (W1, clipped W1, KS, two BC versions). Most strata fail at the project rule or at W1 ≤ 2 pax; they pass at BC ≥ 0.90 (91 %) and W1 ≤ 5 pax (66 %). F4 (input perturbation) not done.

With identical inputs and schedule, the C engine fits `known_data` clearly better than both GAMA models on occupancy variance, tail mass and decision agreement. It still underestimates the mean occupancy, with a bias of the same size in the high-demand and late strata where the GAMA models also fail. The difference between engines is therefore real in this sample, but the cause (engine logic, capacity handling or random-number use) is not identified here.

## Limitations
Same as in the other report: one run per engine and schedule, normal-fitted reference with the 75-passenger cut, no empirical reference for boardings, alightings or waiting times.
