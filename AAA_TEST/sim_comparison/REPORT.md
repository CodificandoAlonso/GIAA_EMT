# Passenger agents (IA) vs aggregate model (AG): comparison report

Plan: `formal_analysis_plan.md`. Runs: AG `SIMULATION_20261012_170524`, IA `SIMULATION_20261012_172453` (1,000 replicas each, same
route, schedule and inputs, byte-identical `INPUTS`). Empirical reference: `WEB_DEVEL/data/known_data`, used as is.
Everything is regenerated by `src/run_all.py`. Pre-specified margins and thresholds are in `src/config.py` and at the top of every log.

## Short answers

| | Answer | Evidence |
|---|---|---|

IA trips last 26 s longer. | Table 2, B2, B4 |

## Outcome (plan section 13)

**Outcome D (both fit poorly) combined with Outcome A for the IA vs AG question.**
IA and AG are practically interchangeable, and AG is about 4 times cheaper. That choice does not matter much for occupancy fidelity,
because neither model matches the empirical distributions in a large share of strata. Agreement between IA and AG does not establish that
either is reliable. The empirical misfit is a limitation of the current model structure, calibration or reference, not of the passenger representation.

## Findings by analysis

### A. Data integrity (Table 1, `logs/table1_data_quality.log`)
- Both runs are complete: 163 trips and 2,202 visits per replica, no duplicates, missing values or failed replicas. Same scheduled service.
- **IA loses passengers** in 887 visits (0.04 %, 589 replicas, about 6 passengers per replica): the bus ends with fewer passengers than previous + boarded − alighted. AG conserves flows exactly. Kept in all analyses.
- **Waiting-time inconsistency:** 5.2 % of IA waits are longer than the time since a previous bus that had room, against 2.0 % in AG. In AG the excess is about one dwell time; in IA it is minutes (p50 266 s, p90 1,118 s), mostly when two buses pass less than 2 min apart. Cause identified from the boarding protocol (passengers must answer the arrival broadcast within 3 cycles).

### B. IA vs AG
- **B1 (Table 2):** 24 of 26 KPIs with a margin are equivalent at the global level (see the table for all 32). Occupancy (mean, SD, quantiles, shares), travel time, boardings and alightings per visit, median wait, bunching and crowding shares are equivalent. IA has lower waiting tails and fewer denied boardings, but the CI for denied boardings per day (−56.7 to −25.3) and for waiting p99 (−80 to −41) reaches beyond the margin on one side.
- **B2:** pooled occupancy W1(IA, AG) = 0.13 pax and BC = 1.000; all 475 strata have W1 ≤ 2 pax (max 1.70) and BC ≥ 0.983. Boardings, alightings and denied per visit: pooled W1 below 0.03. Waiting time: pooled W1 = 4.9 s, 85 % of strata within 30 s; IA waits are shorter in the upper tail (p99 −62 s).
- **B3:** not applicable. The replicas are not matched (different seeds and different consumption of random numbers), so replicas are treated as independent samples.
- **B4 (Fig 5):** 458 of 475 strata are equivalent in mean occupancy and 17 are inconclusive (largest |IA−AG| = 1.62 pax). The 17 concentrate in late slots (20, 22) and in toB, where each stratum has few trips. Peak strata: 100 % equivalent.

### C. Empirical validation (Table 3, Figs 1–2, `c3_travel_time_error.png`)
- **C1, occupancy:** 475 strata. Mean |mean error| 4.6 pax; W1 median 6.8; accepted by the project rule 37.9 % (IA) / 36.2 % (AG); 32.7 % for AG with terminals excluded, identical to the pipeline's own summary (`bhatt_summary.json`). Worst in slots 5, 7, 9 and 18–23 (mean error +5.4 pax in slot 7, −12 pax in slot 23) and in high-demand strata (accepted 1.3 % by W1 ≤ 2).
  The models put a large mass at O = 80 (the capacity cap), which the fitted normal does not have (Fig 1), and underestimate the mean in late slots.
- **Primary estimand Δ_s = W1_IA − W1_AG:** mean −0.073 pax (CI −0.131 to −0.018); IA is closer in 58 % of strata; peak −0.181 (CI −0.297 to −0.086). Statistically distinguishable from zero but about 1 % of the error and 27 times smaller than the 2 pax tolerance: not operationally relevant.
- **C2:** no empirical reference exists for boardings and alightings; not validated here (alightings are externally imposed).
- **C3, travel times:** 464 segment-slot strata with data. MAE 2.72 s (IA) and 2.67 s (AG), mean error +1.1 s, coverage of the 95 % interval 94.9 %, 26–29 strata with |mean error| > 10 s. This validates the sampling and calibration procedure, since the simulator draws times from these distributions.
- **C4:** waiting time has no empirical reference; derived output, not validated.
- **C5:** the same ordering at global, peak, high-demand and mean ≥ 40 levels; the error is largest where it matters operationally (strata with mean ≥ 40: W1 8.2–8.3, P(O ≥ 60) error 11.7 pp).

### D. Operational risk (Table 4, Fig 3)
- **D1:** P(O ≥ 60) is 9.3 % (IA), 9.5 % (AG) and 5.6 % (empirical); P(O ≥ 80): 2.2 % / 2.3 % / 0.5 %. IA − AG is equivalent within ±2 pp in every scope (global, peak, off-peak). Stratified |error| against the empirical normal exceeds 10 pp in 76 strata for both models; IA − AG exceeds 5 pp in none.
  Caveat: the empirical reference is a fitted normal and the project cuts occupancy at 75, so the empirical tail is itself uncertain.
- **D2:** per day, IA has 37.9 visits with denied boarding (AG 40.2) and 323 denied passengers (AG 364, −11 %). Top-10 cells overlap 9/10; Spearman 0.96 over cells. Concentrated in slots 7–8 at toA stops 2539, 2535 and 3903.
- **D3:** persistence is the same (P(run ≥ 2 buses) 19 % in both). Queue size cannot be reconstructed (arrival times are not logged).
- **D4:** occupancy p90/p95/p99/CVaR95 and waiting p90/p95/CVaR95 are equivalent; waiting p99 inconclusive. Daily denied boardings p95 / p99: IA 635 / 907, AG 749 / 1,081.
- **D5:** rule P(O ≥ 60) ≥ 0.10: IA vs AG agree on 98.7 % of strata (κ = 0.97, top-10 identical). Against the empirical reference, both agree on 84.4 % (κ ≈ 0.60) with 13–14 false negatives and 60–61 false positives, and top-10 overlap 7/10.

### E. Cost (Table 5, Fig 6)
- Simulation phase 36 m 13 s (AG) vs 2 h 26 m 53 s (IA): S = 4.06, R = 75.3 %; whole pipeline S = 3.82. Throughput 1,657 against 408 replicas/hour. Sequential runs on the same machine (8 cores); IA's simulation phase started after AG's pipeline ended.
- Output size is the same (126 MB RESULTS + 112 MB PASSENGERS per run). Single measurement, startup and writing included, no CI. CPU, memory and energy were not requested and are not reported; no scaling experiment exists.
- The extra 1.84 h buys a 0.07 pax reduction in mean W1 (1 % of the error).

### F. Robustness (Table 6)
- F1/F2: IA − AG differences and Δ_s are within ±0.2 pax in all 16 groups (direction, peak, demand tertile, route position, boarding and alighting intensity from the inputs). The IA advantage is largest in peak strata (−0.18, CI excludes 0) and not detectable off-peak.
- F3: IA is closer than AG under every metric tested (W1, clipped W1, KS, normal and project BC), always by about 1 % of the error, and the accepted shares differ by less than 1.7 pp under every threshold. Thresholds between 0.90 and 0.99 for BC and between 1 and 5 pax for W1 do not change the conclusion that most strata fail.
- F4 (input perturbation) needs new simulations: not done. F5: one route, working days, calibration-period validation only.

## Statistical vs operational significance
- IA − AG differences are often statistically distinguishable from zero (1,000 replicas give narrow CIs) but are far inside the practical margins: they would not change a capacity or scheduling decision. The exceptions are the IA protocol artefacts (waiting tails, denied boardings, 6 lost passengers per replica), which are small and in the opposite direction to a gain in fidelity.
- Model − empirical differences are both statistically and operationally relevant: they change which strata are flagged (60 extra flags) and overstate full-bus risk about fourfold at c = 80.

## Limitations
- The reference is a fitted normal per stop and slot, not raw observations, and the validation is within the calibration period.
- Replicas are not matched; waiting time and denied boardings have no empirical reference.
- The cause of the empirical misfit (capacity cap versus the normal, late-slot schedule sparsity, calibration) has not been isolated here; the data above are compatible with several explanations.
